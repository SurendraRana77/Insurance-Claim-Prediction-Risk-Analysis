1. Core Objective & Scope

Major Academic Capstone Project

The Insurance Claim Prediction & Risk Analysis System is an end-to-end decision-support prototype designed to evaluate policyholder underwriting profiles and estimate the likelihood of an insurance claim occurring within an annual policy term.

Key Conceptual Distinctions:

  • Probability vs. Severity: The system models event occurrence likelihood, not monetary payout amounts or loss reserve valuations.
  • Claim Occurrence vs. Fraud: A predicted claim reflects genuine insured risk, not fraudulent intent or deceptive behavior.
  • Decision Support vs. Actuarial Rate-Making: Risk tiers are illustrative application boundaries and do not constitute certified actuarial calculations.

3. Machine Learning Methodology

Preprocessing, candidate modeling, and evaluation

Data Leakage Prevention

Post-incident variables (such as repair costs, claim status, or settlement identifiers) are strictly pruned from training data. All transformers (scalers and encoders) are fitted strictly on the 80% training split.

Class Imbalance Handling

Insurance claim data naturally exhibits heavy skew (~20% claims). Rather than optimizing for deceptive raw accuracy, models utilize balanced class weights and are benchmarked on ROC-AUC, Recall, and F1-Score.

Model Interpretability

Individual predictions are complemented with local feature impact analysis. For Logistic Regression, log-odds contributions indicate whether specific factors (such as annual mileage or credit score) pushed risk higher or lower.

4. Ethical Underwriting & System Limitations

Guidelines for responsible AI decision-support in financial services

Correlation vs. Causality: A statistical correlation between a policyholder attribute (e.g., credit rating or age) and claim incidence does not prove direct causation. Models should never be used as sole automated decision-makers without human underwriting oversight.

Automated decision tools in insurance must comply with non-discrimination laws and fair lending guidelines. Underwriters must ensure that protected demographic characteristics do not produce disparate impact or penalize historically marginalized groups.
